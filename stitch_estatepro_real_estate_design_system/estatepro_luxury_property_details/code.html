Design Philosophy

Tactile Minimalism Meets Historic Sanctuary

Arch Monograph No. 41

Perched along the historic forested ridges of Higashiyama, The Sugi Pavilion represents an uncompromising intersection between 400-year-old Japanese temple carpentry and 21st-century seismic structural engineering. Designed directly under the stewardship of Kengo Kuma & Associates, the residence is built around an ancient Japanese cedar (Cryptomeria japonica) grove, oriented precisely toward the serene western panorama of Kyoto's skyline and distant mountain ranges.

Every structural bay uses handcrafted Yakisugi (charred timber) external panels, seasoned over five seasons to withstand Kyoto’s seasonal humidity cycles without synthetic lacquers. Structural thresholds are carved from monolithic slabs of Honshu blue granite, seamlessly merging interior heated flooring with moss gardens curated by a 14th-generation Kyoto landscape master.
